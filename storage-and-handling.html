Receiving a shipment

  • Check that the tamper-evident packaging is intact before opening it.
  • Match the lot on each vial to the lot documentation included with the order, and to its record in the COA Library.
  • Move lyophilized material to its stated storage condition promptly. The dry form tolerates the short ambient periods of transit far better than a solution would; it is extended time at room temperature that storage avoids.
  • Record the date of receipt against the lot number.
  • If anything arrives damaged, or does not match its documentation, report it to us before use. See Returns.

Storing lyophilized material

  • Cold. The stated condition for our lyophilized peptides is −20 °C. A manual-defrost freezer holds temperature more steadily than a frost-free one, whose defrost cycles periodically warm its contents.
  • Dry. Lyophilized powder readily absorbs moisture, and moisture is the main driver of degradation in the solid state. Keep vials sealed, in a closed container with desiccant.
  • Dark. Some amino-acid residues are sensitive to light. Keep vials in their carton or an opaque container.
  • Sealed. Keep each vial stoppered until it is needed. Residues such as methionine, cysteine and tryptophan are prone to oxidation, so exposure to air should be kept short.

Opening a vial

  • Let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening it, ideally in a desiccator, so condensation does not form on the powder.
  • Open vials in a clean, low-humidity area, wearing the protective equipment your laboratory requires.
  • Reseal promptly and return the vial to its storage condition.
  • Material in solution is considerably less stable than the lyophilized powder. Prepare and store solutions according to your laboratory's validated protocol, and avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Laboratory reagents

Bacteriostatic Water is supplied as a liquid and stored at room temperature, as its specification sheet states. Its lot report is a solvent analysis that measures the benzyl-alcohol content. It does not cover sterility or endotoxin; see the report scope table.

How materials ship

Orders are packed to protect temperature-sensitive material in transit, and cold-pack and insulated packaging is available on request. Methods, rates and dispatch times are on the Shipping page.

Safety data, hazards and disposal

We do not currently publish per-product Safety Data Sheets (see SDS status). Qualified laboratory personnel are responsible for hazard assessment, protective equipment, handling and disposal, in accordance with their institution's procedures and applicable regulations. If your organization needs further technical documentation for a lot, contact technical support.
